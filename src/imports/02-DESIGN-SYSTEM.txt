# Radar Design System

Version: 1.0  
Design source: `06-RADAR.tokens.json`  
Web implementation: `07-radar-tokens.css`

## 1. Brand core

### Working identity

- Full name: **NUS Opportunity Radar**
- Short name: **Radar**
- Tagline: **Find your signal.**
- Product promise: **Know what fits, what it takes, and what to do next.**
- Landing-page descriptor: **The opportunity planner built around you.**

### Personality

Radar is:

- **Clear, not crowded.** It reduces noise instead of celebrating volume.
- **Credible, not institutional.** It feels dependable without pretending to be
  an official NUS service.
- **Optimistic, not hyped.** It invites exploration without “AI magic” claims.
- **Student-native, not childish.** It is warm and direct, but serious about
  deadlines and decisions.
- **Forward-looking, not predictive theatre.** Forecasting is explicit about
  what is known, expected, and uncertain.

### Signature idea: signal over noise

Use a restrained radar vocabulary: a single dot, partial concentric arcs, a
sweep line, and small directional marks. The motif may appear in onboarding,
the forecast, loading, empty states, and campaign assets. It must never obstruct
content or become a background on every card.

The visual system is an **editorial utility**: strong hierarchy, short labels,
warm neutral space, precise dates, and one obvious next action.

### Brand safety

Unless permission is granted:

- Do not use the NUS crest, seal, wordmark, or official app chrome.
- Do not call listings “NUS verified” unless NUS performed the verification.
- Prefer `Official source` or `Organiser verified`, which describe the evidence.
- Include `Student-built for NUS students — not an official NUS service` in the
  landing footer and About surface.

## 2. Design principles

### P1 — Lead with the decision

Every screen should answer the user's next question. Cards prioritise deadline,
title, organiser/source, fit, and action; secondary metadata follows.

### P2 — Show the evidence

Every recommendation, date, review summary, or forecast has an explanation or
source one interaction away. Trust information sits near the primary action,
not in a remote footer.

### P3 — Calm urgency

Urgent deadlines become more prominent, not more dramatic. Use clear labels,
exact dates, and one warning color. No countdown animation, pulsing red, or
shame-based copy.

### P4 — One object, many views

An opportunity has one data model and one component family across For You,
Explore, Ask Radar, Plan, notifications, and the widget. Content must not change
between surfaces.

### P5 — AI stays inside the product logic

Ask Radar is a route into the catalogue, not a disconnected chatbot. It returns
the same opportunity cards, cites the same structured facts, and never overrides
eligibility or source data.

### Semantic roles

- Canvas: `neutral-50`.
- Surface: `neutral-0`; quiet surface: `neutral-25`; subdued: `neutral-100`.
- Primary text: `navy-950`; body text: `neutral-900`; secondary: `neutral-600`.
- Primary action: `navy-900`, hover/pressed `navy-800`, white content.
- Link and focus: `blue-700`.
- Signal accent: `orange-600`; use the darker `orange-700` only for short signal
  text on light surfaces. Do not use orange for body copy.
- Verified/strong match: teal pair.
- Deadline warning: amber pair.
- Error/cancelled: red pair.

### Color rules

- Meaning never depends on color alone; pair every semantic color with text or
  an icon.
- Use no more than one semantic tint in a single compact component.
- Never color an entire opportunity card according to category.
- Category identity uses icon + text; an optional 4 px accent mark may use a
  restrained category color, but the card remains neutral.
- A primary button is navy, not orange. Orange is a signal, not a large CTA fill.
- Do not use gradients in the application UI. Campaign artwork may use flat
  overlapping radar arcs, not mesh or aurora gradients.
- Light mode is required for the assignment prototype. Dark mode is optional
  only after light-mode acceptance checks pass.

## 4. Typography

### Family

Use **Manrope** for interface and marketing text, with the fallback stack:

`Manrope, Inter, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", sans-serif`

Use system monospace only for debugging—not for match scores or dates. Enable
tabular numerals for countdowns, rating distributions, and aligned dates.

### Scale

| Style | Size / line height | Weight | Use |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Display | 40 / 48 px | 700 | Landing hero only |
| Heading 1 | 32 / 40 px | 700 | Major page title |
| Heading 2 | 24 / 32 px | 700 | Screen section title |
| Heading 3 | 20 / 28 px | 650 | Card group or sheet title |
| Title | 18 / 24 px | 650 | Opportunity title |
| Body large | 18 / 28 px | 400 | Landing lead, key explanation |
| Body | 16 / 24 px | 400 | Default readable content |
| Label | 14 / 20 px | 650 | Buttons, tabs, fields, chips |
| Small | 14 / 20 px | 400 | Metadata and helper text |
| Caption | 12 / 16 px | 600 | Overline, provenance timestamp |

Rules:

- Never render essential text below 14 px; 12 px is for short secondary labels.
- Opportunity titles use sentence case and clamp to two lines on cards.
- Avoid all caps except very short status overlines of 12 characters or fewer.
- Use real typographic hierarchy, not repeated bold text.

## 5. Spacing and layout

Use a 4 px base grid. Available steps:

`0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 80 px`.

Do not introduce intermediate spacing unless needed for an operating-system safe
area.

### Mobile reference

- Artboard: 390 × 844 px reference; support 320 px minimum.
- Horizontal gutter: 16 px.
- App-bar height: 56 px plus safe area.
- Bottom navigation: 64 px plus safe area.
- Vertical screen-section gap: 32 px.
- Card internal padding: 16 px.
- Dense list-row padding: 12 px vertical, 16 px horizontal.
- Sticky bottom action sits above navigation/safe area and never covers content.

### Tablet and desktop

- 768–1023 px: maximum app content width 720 px, centred; bottom navigation may
  remain for prototype consistency.
- 1024 px and above: use a 240 px left navigation rail and a content column up to
  760 px; optional contextual rail up to 320 px. Do not stretch opportunity text
  across the viewport.
- Landing page: 12-column grid, 24 px gutters, 1200 px maximum container.

## 6. Shape, borders, and elevation

### Radius

- 8 px: compact inline elements and thumbnails.
- 12 px: fields, buttons, list cards.
- 16 px: standard opportunity cards and panels.
- 24 px: bottom sheets and large feature panels.
- Full/pill: status chips only. Do not turn every control into a pill.

### Borders

- Default: 1 px `border-default`.
- Selected/focus-supporting structural border: 2 px brand/focus color without
  changing layout size.
- Avoid internal dividers when spacing alone creates adequate grouping.

### Elevation

- Level 0: no shadow; default for most cards.
- Level 1: subtle shadow for sticky action bars and floating filter/sort sheets.
- Level 2: modal or bottom sheet only.
- Never stack elevated cards inside elevated cards.

## 7. Iconography and imagery

- Use Lucide outline icons at 20 px by default, 16 px in metadata, 24 px in
  bottom navigation.
- Stroke width: 1.75–2 px, consistent within a screen.
- Icon-only controls require an accessible label and a 44 px target.
- Use icons to aid recognition, never to replace unclear labels.
- Opportunity thumbnails are optional. Prefer a simple organiser logo or a
  category illustration on a neutral surface. Never use arbitrary stock photos
  just to fill a card.
- Radar artwork uses two line widths maximum and one orange signal dot.

## 8. Motion

- Fast: 120 ms for press and small feedback.
- Base: 180 ms for state change, chip/filter selection, and save feedback.
- Slow: 260 ms for bottom sheet or screen-level reveal.
- Easing: standard `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`; exit
  `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1)`.
- No looping scan animation, pulsing deadline, autoplay carousel, or motion that
  suggests live AI processing when the prototype is deterministic.
- Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, remove nonessential transforms and use near-zero
  duration without hiding state changes.

## 9. Core composition patterns

### Page hierarchy

1. Page title or concise greeting.
2. Optional one-line context or control.
3. Primary task module.
4. Secondary sections in descending decision value.

Do not lead with a decorative hero inside the signed-in app.

### Card hierarchy

1. Deadline state and absolute deadline.
2. Opportunity title.
3. Organiser + source status.
4. Match label and up to two reasons.
5. Category/date/location metadata.
6. Review summary, if sufficient.
7. Save or open action.

### Progressive disclosure

- Cards help users shortlist; detail pages help them decide.
- `Why this matches` is a sheet or disclosure, not a paragraph on every card.
- Full eligibility, review distribution, source notes, and forecast basis appear
  on detail or secondary sheets.

### Controls

- One primary action per view or action region.
- Secondary action: outlined or text button.
- Tertiary action: text/icon only.
- Destructive action is never adjacent to the default primary action without a
  clear confirmation.
- Minimum target: 44 × 44 px. Default field/button height: 44 px; large CTA 52 px.

## 10. Content design

### Voice

- Direct: `Save and remind me`, not `Leverage smart reminders`.
- Specific: `Checked 28 Aug 2026`, not `Recently verified`.
- Honest: `Expected in October`, not `Coming in October` when dates are unknown.
- Encouraging: `Two profile details would improve your matches`, not `Your
  profile is incomplete`.

### Preferred labels

- `For You`, `Explore`, `Plan`, `Profile`.
- `Why this matches`.
- `Official source`, `Organiser verified`, `Community submitted`.
- `Open official application`.
- `Save`, `Saved`, `Set reminder`, `Mark as applied`.
- `Confirmed`, `Expected`, `Dates not announced`.
- `Ask Radar`, not `AI Opportunity Assistant` in primary navigation.

### Dates and deadlines

- Absolute format: `2 Sep 2026, 11:59 PM SGT`.
- Relative urgency is secondary: `Closes in 5 days`.
- Programme range: `12–14 Sep 2026`.
- Always include timezone for application deadlines.
- Never use `Tomorrow` alone or ambiguous numeric dates such as `02/09/26`.
- For rolling deadlines say `Rolling — apply early`, with the source checked date.

### Ratings and reviews

- Format `4.6 · 38 reviews`, not a lone `4.6`.
- Show `92% would recommend` only with the response count.
- Say `Not enough reviews yet` below three reviews.
- Do not label every review `verified`; state exactly what was verified.

### Match language

- Always pair `95% match` with `Strong match` and `Why this matches`.
- Helper: `Profile fit, not chance of acceptance.`
- Do not use `Perfect match`, `guaranteed`, or `best for you`.

### Empty and error copy

- Say what happened, what is preserved, and what the user can do.
- Empty feed: `No strong matches yet. Add another interest or explore everything.`
- Search empty: `No opportunities match all 4 filters.` Actions: `Clear filters`
  and `Watch this search`.
- Stale source: `We could not re-check this listing. Last confirmed 18 Aug 2026.`
- Closed: `Applications closed 25 Aug 2026.` Offer `Find similar`.

## 11. Trust and responsible AI rules

- Source status and last checked date appear before or immediately after the
  primary application action.
- Generated summaries never replace source facts. Structured dates and
  eligibility win in any conflict.
- Ask Radar responses cite the opportunity cards they use and offer `View source`.
- Never fabricate a listing, review, eligibility rule, organiser, or deadline.
- Prototype AI responses and forecasts are labelled `Demo` in developer/demo
  mode without cluttering the consumer UI.
- Let users correct profile factors, hide recommendations, report stale data,
  and clear assistant history.
- Do not use faculty, year, nationality, disability, or financial-aid details to
  infer competitiveness or likelihood of acceptance.

## 12. Accessibility

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA for text and interactive controls.
- Visible `:focus-visible` ring: 3 px blue with 2 px canvas offset.
- Keyboard order follows reading order; sheets trap focus and return it to the
  opener; Escape closes dismissible overlays.
- Every icon-only control has an accessible name.
- Selected tabs/chips expose state programmatically.
- Announce saved/status/reminder updates with a polite live region.
- Error summaries use `role="alert"` and connect to invalid fields.
- Do not rely on swipe, hover, drag, or color as the only way to act or understand.
- Support 200% zoom and text resizing without clipped CTA labels.
- Provide reduced-motion behavior and meaningful landmark/heading structure.
- Review stars have text equivalents; match gauges have a label and value.

## 13. Do / do not

Do:

- Use warm neutral canvas, white surfaces, navy actions, and one orange signal.
- Put deadline, source, match explanation, and next action within easy reach.
- Reuse the same opportunity card data across every discovery route.
- Use a timeline to make confirmed and expected windows visibly different.
- Keep mobile screens focused on one job.

Do not:

- Build a generic purple AI dashboard.
- Use glass, neon glow, aurora gradients, or a floating chatbot on every screen.
- Hide source freshness at the bottom of a long page.
- make all metadata a colorful pill.
- Let the bell open Forecast or let a widget open an unrelated Home screen.
- Invent official NUS branding, partnership, live data, or predictive accuracy.
