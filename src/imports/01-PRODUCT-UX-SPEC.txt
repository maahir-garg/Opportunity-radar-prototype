# Opportunity Radar — Product and UX specification

Version: 1.0  
Status: Prototype direction; user-research findings are not yet complete  
Primary platform: Mobile-first responsive web app / PWA  
Primary audience: Time-constrained NUS undergraduates, initially Years 2–3

## Product statement

NUS Opportunity Radar helps students find the right opportunities before they
close. It brings internships, research, exchanges, competitions, hackathons,
entrepreneurship programmes, volunteering, talks, and grants into one consistent
experience; recommends a manageable set based on a student's profile and goals;
shows the evidence needed to judge fit and trustworthiness; and turns saved
opportunities into a simple deadline plan.

## Positioning

Radar is not another application portal and should not pretend to replace
official NUS or organiser sources. It is the **decision and action layer** above
those sources.

Product promise: **Know what fits, what it takes, and what to do next.**

## Current problem hypotheses

These are assumptions until the team completes and documents interviews:

1. Students discover opportunities through disconnected email, Telegram,
   social, university, faculty, and organiser channels.
2. High-volume channels make personally relevant items hard to identify.
3. Students delay or miss applications because deadlines and next actions are
   spread across different places.
4. Students lack credible, comparable context about eligibility, commitment,
   organiser quality, and past student experience.

The prototype should help test these assumptions; it must not present them as
already validated findings.

## Three primary goals

G1 — **Discover relevant opportunities quickly.** Centralise discovery and
reduce noise through profile-aware recommendations, search, and filters.

G2 — **Decide with confidence.** Make fit, provenance, freshness, eligibility,
commitment, and student experience easy to inspect.

G3 — **Act before the deadline.** Connect saving to a clear status, next action,
reminder, and 30-day view.

Trust is also a system-wide quality bar: every surface that makes a recommendation
or deadline claim must explain its source.

## MVP feature set

The ten original ideas are consolidated into six meaningful product features so
the prototype remains focused and testable.

F1 — **Profile and preferences**

- Fast first-use setup: year, faculty/school, up to three interests, desired
  outcomes, and optional eligibility facts.
- Editable at any time.
- Profile factors used in recommendations are visible and removable.

F2 — **Personalised discovery**

- `For You` feed plus `Explore` search, category filters, `Eligible for me`, and
  `Closing soon`.
- One shared opportunity catalogue and taxonomy.
- Users can hide a recommendation or say why it is not relevant.

F3 — **Explainable match**

- Compact score/label on cards and detail.
- `Why this matches` shows satisfied factors, unknown factors, and possible
  blockers.
- The score is a ranking aid based on structured prototype rules, not a
  probability of acceptance or success.

F4 — **Decision-ready opportunity detail**

- Standardised summary, organiser, source, last checked, application deadline,
  programme dates, eligibility, commitment, location/mode, and official link.
- Structured student reviews with cohort context and review count.
- Report outdated or incorrect information.

F5 — **Plan, forecast, and reminders**

- Save, set a status, record one next action, and choose reminder timing.
- Plan tabs: Saved, Preparing, Applied, Past.
- 30-day Radar separates currently confirmed openings/deadlines from expected
  seasonal windows.

F6 — **Ask Radar**

- Natural-language, goal-based discovery inside Explore.
- Returns catalogue-backed result cards with match reasons and source links.
- Uses deterministic mock replies in the prototype and never invents eligibility
  or deadline facts.

Stretch S1 — **Lock-screen widget**

- Shows one opted-in saved opportunity or daily best match.
- Deep-links directly to that opportunity, not to Home.
- Requires notification/widget permissions and must have an unavailable state.
- Do not build until the MVP journeys work.

## Goal-to-feature traceability

| Goal | Supporting features | Main surfaces | Testable outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 Discover | F1, F2, F3, F6 | Onboarding, For You, Explore, Ask Radar | Student finds one suitable item and can explain why it appeared |
| G2 Decide | F3, F4 | Opportunity detail, Why Match, Reviews | Student identifies source, eligibility, commitment, deadline, and one trust signal without help |
| G3 Act | F4, F5 | Detail, reminder sheet, Plan, Radar forecast | Student saves an item, sets a reminder/next action, and later finds it in the correct status |

## Canonical information architecture

Bottom navigation has four destinations:

1. **For You** — recommended and closing-soon opportunities; forecast preview.
2. **Explore** — conventional search, categories, filters, and Ask Radar.
3. **Plan** — saved/application states, next actions, reminders, and 30-day view.
4. **Profile** — preferences, eligibility facts, notifications, privacy, About.

The top-bar bell always opens the notification inbox. Opportunity detail, Why
Match, filters, reminder setup, reviews, and Ask Radar are child surfaces.

## Opportunity truth model

Keep these state families separate in data and UI.

Opportunity availability:

- `upcoming`, `open`, `closingSoon`, `closed`, `full`, `cancelled`.

User progress:

- `none`, `saved`, `preparing`, `applied`, `completed`, `dismissed`.

Forecast status:

- `confirmed` — tied to a current official listing or date.
- `expected` — a possible seasonal window based on stated historical evidence.
- `watching` — user requested an alert; not a prediction confidence.

Source status:

- `official`, `organiserVerified`, `communitySubmitted`, `needsReview`.

Never infer user progress from opportunity availability. Never treat an expected
forecast item as an open opportunity.

## Required opportunity fields

- Stable ID, title, organiser, category ID, tags, short summary.
- Source status, source label, source URL, last checked timestamp.
- Availability state, application deadline with timezone, programme dates.
- Location and mode.
- Eligibility requirements and unknown/conditional requirements.
- Estimated time commitment with provenance.
- Match score/label, satisfied reasons, missing facts, and blockers.
- Rating average, count, recommendation percentage, and structured reviews.
- User progress, next action, reminder date, and last user update.
- Forecast status and basis, if applicable.

## Match model for the prototype

Use a deterministic weighted demonstration model only:

- Eligibility satisfied: 40 points.
- Interest/category alignment: 25 points.
- Goal alignment: 20 points.
- Timing/availability alignment: 10 points.
- Location/mode preference: 5 points.

Display the numeric value only with a plain-language label:

- 85–100: `Strong match`
- 70–84: `Good match`
- 50–69: `Possible match`
- Below 50: do not place in `Top matches` unless the user explicitly searches.

If a hard eligibility blocker is known, show `Eligibility issue` instead of a
high match label regardless of the interest score. `Why this matches` must list
the factors used. The UI must say the score measures profile fit, not admission
likelihood.

## Forecast model for the prototype

The forecast is a planning surface, not generative fortune-telling.

- Confirmed items show exact current dates and official source.
- Expected items show an approximate window, the previous occurrence used as
  evidence, and `Dates not announced`.
- Never mix confirmed and expected marks without labels and a legend.
- Never show unexplained `High / Medium / Low` values.
- A user can watch an expected item and receive a mock confirmation notice.

## Review model

- Reviews are written by NUS students in the concept; label whether attendance
  was verified or only student identity was verified.
- Show reviewer context useful for relevance: year, faculty/school, participation
  year, and optional anonymous name.
- Structured fields: overall rating, would recommend, commitment accuracy,
  organiser communication, best for, and free-text note.
- Always show review count and freshness. A sparse state must say `Not enough
  reviews yet`; never imply confidence from one review.
- Include report and moderation states. Do not expose personal contact details.

## External application handoff

Radar does not submit applications in the MVP.

1. `Open official application` shows the destination domain and leaves Radar.
2. Returning users see `Did you apply?` with `Mark as applied` and `Not yet`.
3. The user can mark applied manually and undo it.
4. A failed or unavailable link shows the last checked date and report action.

## Acquisition and feasibility assumptions

Potential distribution: student societies, faculty channels, NUS Enterprise/
CFG/GRO programme owners, orientation, and shareable opportunity links. The
landing page should collect interest without claiming institutional partnership.

The real product would need current structured data, source permission, provider
verification, moderation, privacy consent, and a reliable update workflow. For
the assignment prototype, use the supplied fictional data and label it as demo
content. Never scrape or claim a live integration.

## Out of scope

- Hosting complete applications inside Radar.
- Resume or application writing.
- Open social networking, direct messaging, or follower feeds.
- Automated acceptance-likelihood or competitiveness predictions.
- Unmoderated reviews.
- Expansion beyond NUS.
- A live LLM or live opportunity crawler.
- The OS widget before the core prototype is complete.

## Prototype research questions

Use the prototype to observe, not merely ask whether users “like” it:

1. Can students identify why a recommendation fits them?
2. Which trust evidence do they inspect before deciding?
3. Do they understand `Expected` versus `Confirmed` in the forecast?
4. Can they save, choose a next action, and later retrieve the item unaided?
5. Is conventional search or Ask Radar the more natural starting point?
6. Does the match percentage clarify relevance or create false confidence?
